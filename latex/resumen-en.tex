This Master's Thesis presents the design, development, and evaluation of a comprehensive 
system aimed at automating medical documentation by transforming voice dictations 
into standardized clinical reports. 
The system proposes a workflow pipeline that outlines the following stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
    \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
    \setlength{\topsep}{4pt}
    \item \textbf{Secure audio capture:} 
        Implementation of a client-server architecture with end-to-end encryption 
        to guarantee data integrity.
    \item \textbf{Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR):} 
        Audio transcription using artificial intelligence models adapted to the 
        medical lexicon.
    \item \textbf{Extraction and normalization of clinical entities:}
        Identification, categorization, and normalization of clinical concepts under
        international standards such as SNOMED CT (Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine --
        Clinical Terms) and CIE-10-ES (the Spanish clinical modification of the 10th revision
        of the International Statistical Classification of Diseases).
    \item \textbf{Report generation via LLMs:}
        Information synthesis using Large Language Models from a verified context of
        previously normalized clinical entities, aimed at reducing the risk of fabricating
        information absent from the original dictation.
    \item \textbf{Structured and interoperable output:}
        Exporting results in standard formats (JSON/FHIR, Fast Healthcare Interoperability
        Resources) for direct integration
        into Electronic Health Record (\englishText{EHR}) systems and clinical report generation.
\end{enumerate}

The project is grounded in the principles of privacy by design, ensuring strict
compliance with current legal frameworks (GDPR, the General Data Protection Regulation, and
\englishText{LOPDGDD}, the Spanish Organic Law on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee
of digital rights).
The feasibility of the proposal is validated through feedback from industry
specialists and a quantitative evaluation based on technical accuracy metrics
(\englishText{WER}, Word Error Rate, for transcription and $F_1$ score for entity extraction),
as well as time-efficiency indicators and clinical relevance.
